Features

What is in the current release, window by window, and where it still falls short. The wiki explains how to use each part.

The windows

The layout follows FL Studio: Browser, Channel rack, Piano roll, Playlist and Mixer, each opened and closed with the same keys as there.

Playlist

Pattern, audio and automation clips on tracks. Right-click a clip for split, chop, merge, reverse, normalize and fit to tempo.

  • Several arrangements in one project, markers and time selection
  • Automation clips with curves for any knob or fader, and optional automation lanes under tracks
  • Audio recording with loop takes and comping
  • Video tracks with a timecode ruler (export with video needs FFmpeg)

Channel rack

A step sequencer with one row per instrument. Each channel has volume, pan, mute and solo, and each pattern its own name, colour and length.

  • Swing, cut groups, an arpeggiator and layer channels
  • The PC keyboard plays notes like a piano

Piano roll

Configurable snap, chords and scales, 16 note colours, note groups, and slide and porta notes.

  • Lanes for velocity, pan and pitch under the notes
  • Ghost notes from other channels
  • Quantize, strum, arpeggiate and randomize tools
  • .mid import and export

Mixer

Up to 127 insert tracks. Each has 10 effect slots with dry/wet, sends with levels, a built-in EQ and delay compensation.

  • Sidechain from one menu on any effect with a side input
  • VCA faders and groups that fold
  • Moving a track keeps its sends, sidechains and automation

Automation and controllers

Any knob or fader of a channel, the mixer, an effect or a plugin can become a clip with points and curves. MIDI controllers can be linked to any parameter, and modulators (an LFO or a random source) move a knob around its own value.

Clip-launch layout

An optional layout (View › Layout style) puts a grid of clips and scenes next to the Playlist, in the manner of Ableton Live's Session view. Clips have launch modes, quantisation, legato, loop and follow actions.

Live sets bring their Session clips into this grid, and exporting an .als writes the grid back. The pattern layout stays the default and nothing changes in it.

Projects from other programs

An imported project is saved as a .lynx file, and the original is never changed. After each import a report lists what was approximated or left out.

Instruments and effects

Instruments

  • Sampler with zones, slices, time-stretch and disk streaming
  • LYNX Soundfont, an SF2 / SF3 player
  • Built-in drums: kick, snare, hat, clap
  • Note effects (sequencer, key mapper, keyboard splitter) and controllers (X-Y, envelope, voltage)

Effects

  • EQs, compressor with sidechain, limiter, gate
  • Delays, reverbs, chorus, filters, distortion
  • Vocoder, pitch correction, pitch and frequency shifters
  • Patcher: a node graph of effects, instruments and plugins
  • Hardware insert for outboard gear, with delay compensation

Third-party plugins

  • VST3 and CLAP on every system
  • Audio Units on macOS, LV2 on Linux, VST2 on Windows and Linux
  • 32-bit VST2 and VST3 on Windows, through a bridge
  • A plugin can run in its own process, so a crash in it does not take the project down
  • ASIO is Windows-only

Stand-ins for third-party plugins

LYNX SHARE has its own effects that play the role of widely used third-party plugins. If a project uses one of those plugins and it is not installed, the LYNX SHARE effect loads in its slot and the original's settings are kept for when it is back.

Performance

The audio engine spreads channels and mixer tracks across CPU cores, skips channels that are silent and streams long audio files from disk. Measured against FL Studio 2026 on the developer's PC, it used 2.5 to 7 times less RAM and rendered the demo songs 2 to 3 times faster.
