Features

What is in the current release, window by window, and where it still falls short. The wiki explains how to use each part.

The windows

The default layout follows FL Studio: Browser, Channel rack, Piano roll, Playlist and Mixer, each opened and closed with the same keys as there. The other two layouts, Clip-launch and Simple, are further down.

Playlist

Pattern, audio and automation clips on tracks. Right-click a clip for split, chop, merge, reverse, normalize and fit to tempo.

  • Several arrangements in one project, markers and time selection
  • Automation clips with curves for any knob or fader, and optional automation lanes under tracks
  • Audio recording with loop takes and comping
  • Video tracks with a timecode ruler (export with video needs FFmpeg)
  • Warp markers and Auto-warp, to lock audio clips to the beat
  • FL Studio's 13 automation curve modes on every point
  • A Sections lane that moves, copies and repeats whole song parts (a LYNX extra)
  • Clips own their notes: an instrument track whose clips each keep their own notes, without patterns
  • Hide tracks, or deactivate them to unload their plugins and free CPU and memory (a LYNX extra)
  • Track versions: named alternate versions of a track's clips, lanes and effects (a LYNX extra)
  • A Chord lane with the song's chords, drawn or detected, that channels can follow (a LYNX extra)

Channel rack

A step sequencer with one row per instrument. Each channel has volume, pan, mute and solo, and each pattern its own name, colour and length.

  • Swing, cut groups, an arpeggiator and layer channels
  • The PC keyboard plays notes like a piano
  • Freeze: a channel plays as audio and its instrument is unloaded
  • Automation clips as rows of the rack, with steps that start them and range knobs, as in FL Studio
  • Deactivate: a channel's instrument is unloaded until you need it again

Piano roll

Configurable snap, chords and scales, 16 note colours, note groups, and slide and porta notes.

  • Lanes for velocity, pan and pitch under the notes
  • Ghost notes from other channels
  • Quantize, strum, arpeggiate and randomize tools
  • .mid import and export
  • Note chance, velocity range and Piano roll scripts
  • Limit to a scale or chord, Scale levels, Flip and Randomize, as in FL Studio
  • Key markers that change the key along the song, and a Scale degree chord stamp
  • The Chord helper: related keys and borrowed chords for the selected chord, with favourites
  • Up to five controller lanes stacked at once (a LYNX extra)

Mixer

Up to 500 insert tracks. Each has 10 effect slots with dry/wet, sends with levels, a built-in EQ and delay compensation.

  • Sidechain from one menu on any effect with a side input
  • VCA faders and groups that fold
  • Moving a track keeps its sends, sidechains and automation
  • An optional loudness meter (LUFS and true peak) beside the Master
  • Edison on any mixer slot, recording even with its window closed
  • ARA plugins such as Melodyne on an insert, editing every audio clip routed there

Automation and controllers

Any knob or fader of a channel, the mixer, an effect or a plugin can become a clip with points and curves. MIDI controllers can be linked to any parameter, and modulators move a knob around its own value.

Modulators come in eight kinds: an LFO, an envelope follower, an ADSR envelope, steps, a random source, a macro, keytrack and velocity. Drag one onto a knob, or pick it with Modulate with in a control's menu, also for third-party plugins, and a ring shows the movement. Capture automation turns the moves you made while playing, without recording, into automation afterwards.

Besides FL Studio's Record automation, a LYNX setting writes in Touch, Latch or Write mode, as in other DAWs.

Common MIDI controllers work from profiles (Mackie Control, Launchkey MK3, APC Key 25 mk2, KeyLab Essential mk3 and nanoKONTROL2). An MPE controller plays each note with its own bend, timbre and pressure; this is a first stage. Right-click a control › Link to controller... opens Remote control settings, as in FL Studio, for knobs, notes and buttons.

Polyphonic note editor

Right-click an audio clip in the Playlist and choose Edit notes (polyphonic)... under LYNX extras: LYNX analyses the recording and shows each note of its chords on its own row. Drag a single note up or down to retune it (hold Alt for cents) or sideways to move it in time, while the rest of the chord stays as it was. This is a feature of LYNX's own, with no FL Studio counterpart.

In every layout

Tracker

  • View › Tracker (Shift+F7) shows a pattern's notes as rows and channels, the tracker way
  • It edits the same notes as the Piano roll, so both stay in step
  • Tracker modules (.mod, .xm, .s3m, .it) open with their samples

Finding your way

  • The first start asks where you are coming from and opens the matching layout
  • A start page with your recent projects and the templates
  • Search actions (? or Ctrl+Shift+Y) finds any command, menu item, LYNX extra or Settings page
  • The Settings window has its own search (Ctrl+F)

Projects

  • File › Open in new window: two projects side by side, with copy and paste between them
  • Import tracks from another project, with their clips, inserts and automation
  • Collect all and save copies every sample the project uses next to it
  • Save a copy for an older LYNX, for friends who have not updated yet

Make music together

File › Online session sends a project to a friend as one file with its samples, or starts a live session for up to four people who edit the same project at once, end-to-end encrypted, with a chat. Guests can use plugins the host shares with them, and the host can let someone only listen or remove them from the session.

Clip-launch and Simple layouts

The Clip-launch layout (View › Layout style) works in the style of Ableton Live: a grid of clips and scenes next to the Playlist, like Live's Session view. Clips have launch modes, quantisation, legato, loop and follow actions.

Live sets bring their Session clips into this grid, and exporting an .als writes the grid back. The FL Studio workflow stays the default and nothing changes in it.

A selected clip opens in a Clip View with Clip, Launch and Notes panels and Live 12's MIDI tools. The selected track's effects show as a chain of cards, and racks with chains, zones and macros open from Live sets and are written back. A Session mixer sits under the grid, a Drum Rack plays pads with choke groups and return chains, and MIDI and Key Map Modes link controllers and computer keys to clips, scenes and knobs. Freeze Track and Bounce turn a track into audio, and Ableton Link keeps the tempo in sync with Live and other Link apps.

In the Device View a rack shows its chains' devices inline, and Macro Map mode gives any device parameter to a macro. The Session mixer has Live's Solo / Cue, with a Cue Out for headphones.

The same menu has the third layout, Simple, which mixes BandLab's approachable one-window style with FL Studio Mobile's way of working: one window with the tracks and the timeline, a New track chooser, starter beats, loops that follow the song's key, a 16-pad Sampler, quick recording on a voice track, a track dock with one-knob effects and a Chord pad, a first-song tour and a Finish dock that saves the song as a file. FL Studio Mobile projects (.flm) open in it, and a folder of stems and MIDI from another app comes in with one drop.

In the Simple layout each track also has Reverb and Echo sends and Duck under, which dips it under another track, such as the bass under the drums. Song parts can be marked as sections that move and repeat as a whole, and the ruler's menu changes the time signature mid-song.

The Simple layout's Drum Machine

A Drums track opens on a step grid under the timeline: one row per sound, patterns A to H, length and swing. Click a square to add or remove a hit while the beat plays, or press the row's letter key to play its sound.

Every format and its limits

Instruments and effects

Instruments

  • Sampler with zones, slices, time-stretch and disk streaming
  • LYNX Soundfont, an SF2 / SF3 player
  • Built-in drums: kick, snare, hat, clap
  • Note effects (sequencer, key mapper, keyboard splitter) and controllers (X-Y, envelope, voltage)
  • LYNX Model: physical models of marimba, vibraphone, xylophone, glockenspiel, tubular bells and kalimba
  • Helix, a wavetable synth with granular and spectral layers, and Tetra, a 4-operator FM synth

Effects

  • EQs, compressor with sidechain, limiter, gate
  • Delays, reverbs, chorus, filters, distortion
  • Vocoder, pitch correction, pitch and frequency shifters
  • Patcher: a node graph of effects, instruments and plugins
  • Hardware insert for outboard gear, with delay compensation
  • De-essers, channel strips, a denoiser and a vocal doubler

Third-party plugins

  • VST3 and CLAP on every system
  • JSFX scripts (REAPER's effects and instruments) on every system, with 31 free scripts included
  • Audio Units on macOS, LV2 on Linux, VST2 on Windows and Linux
  • 32-bit VST2 and VST3 on Windows, through a bridge
  • A plugin can run in its own process, so a crash in it does not take the project down
  • ASIO is Windows-only
  • The LYNX plugin pack: 39 of LYNX SHARE's own effects and instruments for other DAWs, as a CLAP (some also as VST3 and, on Linux, LV2), in an optional separate download

FL Studio's plugins, recreated

205 instruments, effects and tools are built into LYNX SHARE 0.1.31.2. Each picture is the plugin's own editor, drawn by the released program. Third-party plugins (VST3, CLAP and others) load next to them.

FL Studio's own plugins cannot be loaded by another program. LYNX SHARE has its own engine for most of them, written from scratch, and reads each plugin's saved settings from the .flp or from an .fst preset. A Sytrus channel opens as LYNX SHARE's Sytrus engine with the same patch, a Fruity Limiter slot as its limiter with the same values.

Some engines need wave data or samples that belong to FL Studio. LYNX SHARE reads those from your own FL Studio installation and ships none of them.

Stand-ins for third-party plugins

LYNX SHARE has its own effects that play the role of widely used third-party plugins. If a project uses one of those plugins and it is not installed, the LYNX SHARE effect loads in its slot and the original's settings are kept for when it is back.

Performance

The audio engine spreads channels and mixer tracks across CPU cores, skips channels that are silent, renders the part of a project you are not playing live a little ahead, and streams long audio files from disk. Measured on one PC (the developer's, Windows 11, Ryzen 5 3600) while FL Studio 2026's own demo song Thief plays, LYNX SHARE 0.1.31 used 34.4 % of one CPU core and 564 MB of private memory, where FL Studio used 905 MB, and it opened the song in 4.3 s (FL Studio: 7.1 s). The method and the numbers are in the 0.1.31 release notes; your results will differ.

Safe overloads, on by default as in FL Studio, keeps the window responsive when the CPU runs out, and a deactivated track or channel costs nothing until you switch it back on. The Linux arm64 build runs on a Raspberry Pi 4 or 5.

Offline and private

LYNX SHARE works without an internet connection, and it connects nowhere before you answer the first-start window, which asks whether to check for updates. There is no account and no telemetry, and a crash report is sent only if you agree. For sandboxes and packagers, LYNX_OFFLINE=1 or --offline turns off every connection of LYNX SHARE's own.
